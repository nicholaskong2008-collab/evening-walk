Most evenings my dad and I go for a walk, and most evenings the conversation ends up on the economy. A headline about interest rates turns into an argument about mortgages. A story about tariffs turns into a debate about who really pays them.

This blog is where I take those conversations further. Each essay starts with a real event, then works back to the economics underneath it: the incentives, the trade-offs, and the numbers that decide who wins and who loses.

## Who I am

I'm Nicholas Kong. I'm most interested in how policy decisions play out in the real world: who a tax actually falls on, why an interest-rate change takes months to reach people's wallets, and what happens when a government's plans meet the incentives of the people they affect. I find an argument easier to trust when I can see the data behind it.

Outside the blog, I'm interning at a financial advisory firm, where I've worked on evaluating investments in technology companies. That work has made me curious about how markets price new ideas, and how much of a company's value rests on assumptions about the wider economy.

I also run a sports podcast, Athlete Archives, which has pulled me towards sports analytics and the economics of sport: how clubs spend, why some leagues are more competitive than others, and what the numbers can and can't tell you about performance.

## How I write these

- I start from a reliable source, usually a major newspaper or official data, and link to it at the end of every essay.
- I try to explain one economic idea well rather than several badly.
- When I use a number, I say where it came from.

If you spot a mistake or disagree with an argument, I'd like to hear it.

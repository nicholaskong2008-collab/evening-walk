Over the past year, American companies' pre-tax profits rose by about $1.2trn. Over the same period, the corporate tax they paid fell sharply. That sounds like a contradiction, but it follows directly from how the tax system treats investment.

{% include stat.html number="$1.2trn" label="Rise in US pre-tax corporate profits over the past year, according to The Economist" %}

## Where the tax went

Firms can deduct investment spending from their taxable profits, and recent rule changes let them deduct more of it up front. The biggest earners right now are also the biggest investors, because tech firms are spending record amounts on AI data centres. High profits minus high deductions leaves a small tax bill.

## The bigger problem: who the tax system taxes

Most rich countries raise the bulk of their revenue from workers, through income tax and social-security contributions. In America, about three-quarters of federal tax revenue comes from labour. If AI shifts income from wages towards profits, the economy can grow while the tax base shrinks.

An AI tax is often suggested as the fix. The numbers show why it would struggle to replace lost labour taxes:

> A tax on AI would be a tax on a base less than a twentieth the size of the one it is meant to replace.

## What I think

The economists quoted in the article lean towards reforming existing taxes: taxing capital gains more like income, taxing excess corporate profits, and relying more on consumption taxes. That seems right to me, because these are harder to avoid than a brand-new tax on AI.
